## The Jevons Effect Framework

**Core Principle:** Scarcity increases perceived value. But REAL scarcity (limited capacity, not fake timers).

### The 4 Pillars:

1. **Jevons Effect (Scarcity)** → "I can take [N] more clients this quarter" (real capacity)
2. **Fear of Loss** → "Without this, your next review exposes the gap"
3. **Urgency** → "Early adopter rate while real slots last"
4. **Strategic Indifference** → "This might not be a fit if you're just exploring"

## Example 1: INTERESTED PROSPECT (High Urgency)

**Their Email:**
> "Hi Herbert, this looks interesting. We need better audit trails for AI model overrides. Can we schedule a call?"

**BAD Response (Eager Seller):**
> "Thanks so much for your interest! I'd love to chat! I'm available anytime this week or next. Just let me know what works best for you. Looking forward to showing you everything Mythara can do!"

❌ Problems: Desperate, no scarcity, no urgency, too available

**GOOD Response (Jevons Effect):**

> Sarah,
> 
> Quick question before we schedule—do you have a model risk review or audit in the next 90 days, or is this exploratory?
> 
> Asking because I have [N] early adopter slots left at $500 (normally $2,500), at my real onboarding capacity — confirm the current number before you send this. After the window closes I'm at full price. Starting now means you'll have months of validated audit history by your next review, instead of starting from zero.

> If you have near-term regulatory pressure: I can do Tuesday 10am or Wednesday 2pm MT for 15 minutes.
> 
> If timing's not urgent, totally understand—just don't want you to miss the $500 window.
> 
> Herbert

✅ Strengths: Qualifies hard, creates scarcity, fear of loss, urgency, indifferent to outcome

## Example 2: TECHNICAL QUESTION (Medium Urgency)

**Their Email:**
> "How does your system handle HIPAA compliance? We need tamper-evident logs for FDA audits."

**BAD Response (Feature Dump):**
> "Great question! Mythara uses SHA-256 cryptographic hashing to create tamper-evident audit trails. We also support role-based access control, encrypted storage, and real-time integrity verification. We integrate with Epic, Cerner, and most EHR systems. Here's a 10-page technical whitepaper..."

❌ Problems: No urgency, no scarcity, technical lecture, no CTA

**GOOD Response (Jevons Effect):**

> Dr. Park,
> 
> Cryptographic hashing (SHA-256) on every AI decision—auditors can verify zero tampering. For example, a hypothetical health system piloting this could point its FDA auditors at passing validation reports generated from real runs.
> 
> Without this, your FDA auditor sees black-box AI decisions with no provable lineage. That is exactly the kind of gap auditors flag.
> 
> The early adopter rate ($500 vs $2,500) holds while slots last — I can confirm current availability when we talk. I can do a 15-min screen share Tuesday or Wednesday, or just send you our validation report from real runs and you can evaluate async.
> 
> Which is faster for you?
> 
> Herbert

✅ Strengths: Answers directly, reframes to loss, hypothetical social-proof framing, urgency, assumptive close

⚠️ Social-proof rule: only cite real customers or pilots with their consent. Never present a hypothetical example as an actual customer.

## Example 3: NOT INTERESTED (Takeaway Selling)

**Their Email:**
> "Thanks for reaching out, but we're not looking at new vendors right now."

> "I totally understand! But what if I told you we could save you 20 hours per week and reduce compliance risk by 80%? Would you be open to just a 10-minute call to learn more? No pressure!"

❌ Problems: Not listening to "no," pushy, desperate

**GOOD Response (Jevons Effect):**

> Mike,
> 
> No problem—appreciate the heads up.
> 
> If your next audit gets flagged for AI explainability, feel free to reach out. I'm only taking new clients through Q1 anyway.
> 
> Quick question—do you know anyone at [competitor bank] handling model risk compliance? Happy to help them out.
> 
> Herbert

✅ Strengths: Total indifference, plants fear seed, scarcity (capacity limit), referral ask, high status

>

> Herbert

✅ Strengths: Forces qualification, creates scarcity, urgency, indifference, assumptive close

## Example 5: PRICING OBJECTION (Reframe to Loss)

**Their Email:**
> "$500 seems expensive for an early adopter price. Can you do $250?"

**BAD Response (Discount Cave):**
> "Let me check with my manager. I might be able to do $350 if you sign by Friday."

❌ Problems: Lowers status, shows desperation, no value justification

**GOOD Response (Jevons Effect):**

> Sarah,
> 
> Fair question. Compared to what?
> 
> A regulatory fine for un-auditable AI decisions, or a failed model risk review with months of remediation work, costs far more than $500 to prevent. The $500 is for companies that can move fast—it's normally $2,500.
> 
> That said, if budget's tight, might make sense to wait until you have an audit forcing the issue. Companies facing regulatory deadlines don't hesitate — that's the pattern this is built for.
> 
> Are you in that boat, or more exploratory?
> 
> Herbert

✅ Strengths: Reframes to ROI, creates FOMO, takes away (willing to walk), qualifies seriousness

> 
> Herbert

✅ Strengths: Creates urgency, plants FOMO, offers value, takeaway close, high status

## The Indifference Mindset

**Key Insight:** The moment you NEED the sale, you lose leverage.

### Mental Frames:

1. **"I'm interviewing them"** (not pitching)
2. **"I have limited capacity"** (true—you can't serve 1000 customers at once)
3. **"Bad-fit customers cost me money"** (also true—support burden)
4. **"I'd rather have no sale than wrong sale"** (quality > quantity)

### Language Patterns:

- "This might not be a fit if..."
- "I'm prioritizing companies with..."
- "If timing's not right, totally understand"
- "I only work with teams that can move fast"
- "Are you in that category or more exploratory?"

---

## Urgency Stack (Layered Scarcity)

Don't use ONE urgency trigger. Stack them:

**Example Close:**

> If you commit before the early adopter window closes:
> - $500 pricing (vs $2,500 after)
> - Priority onboarding (vs my standard queue)
> - I'll personally run your first audit report (vs self-service)
> - You start building audit history ahead of your next review (vs starting from zero)
> 
> Once the window closes, I can't guarantee any of that.
> 
> Tuesday 10am or Wednesday 2pm—which is better?

## Fear of Loss Triggers (Use Sparingly)

### Regulatory Fear:
- "Your next audit without provable AI lineage leaves a gap your auditor can flag"
- "Un-auditable AI decisions are the kind of finding that draws regulatory scrutiny"
- "Model risk guidelines expect this level of documentation — have it before your review"

### Competitive Fear:
- "If a competitor in your space started building audit history now, they'd be ahead of you at review time"
- "While you evaluate, any early adopter is building audit history you won't have"

### Opportunity Cost:
- "Every week without this = manual prep time you can't get back"
- "Early adopters of this will have better audit outcomes than you"

### Pricing Fear:
- "The $500 early adopter rate ends when slots are gone — then it's $2,500. Confirm current availability before you quote a deadline."
- "My onboarding capacity is [N] companies this quarter. When I hit it, it's full price."

---

## When NOT to Use Jevons Effect

❌ Don't use fake scarcity (countdown timers, "only 3 left" when you have unlimited capacity)
❌ Don't be rude or arrogant (indifference ≠ asshole)
❌ Don't use fear-mongering on people who clearly don't have the problem
❌ Don't stack too many urgency triggers (sounds desperate)

✅ Use when you have REAL constraints (time, capacity, pricing tiers)
✅ Use when they have REAL consequences (audits, fines, competitive pressure)
✅ Use when you're willing to walk away from bad-fit customers

## Summary: The Jevons Effect Email Formula

```
1. Acknowledge (1 sentence, don't gush)
2. Qualify hard ("Do you have X or is this exploratory?")
3. Create scarcity ("I have 2 slots left at $500")
4. Fear of loss ("Without this, you risk [specific bad outcome]")
5. Social proof ("for example, early adopters would bank audit data ahead of the curve" — only name real customers)
6. Urgency ("early adopter rate ends when real slots are gone, then it's $2,500")
7. Assumptive close ("Tuesday 10am or Wednesday 2pm?")
8. Indifferent exit ("If timing's not right, no worries")
```

**Tone:** Confident selector. Outcome-independent. High status. Helpful but busy.
